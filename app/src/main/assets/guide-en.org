This notebook shows what Orgwhal can do. Each note explains a feature and is also an example to try. Everything is written in Org: the same file opens in Emacs. Delete the notebook when you no longer need it (long press on it on the home screen).

* Getting started
** The notes folder
Orgwhal reads and writes the =.org= files of a folder on the phone (synced with Syncthing, Nextcloud…). There is no database: a file is a notebook, a heading (=*=) is a note.

- Choose the folder in *Settings → Notes → Folder*.
- Files changed by another app are read again within seconds.
- Pull the list down to reload the folder.
- In a long list or note, scrolling down brings up a small double arrow: tap it to go straight to the bottom. In a notebook, among the sub-notes of an unfolded note, it is a single arrow: a tap goes to the end of those sub-notes (tap again for the end of the note around them), a long press to the bottom. A tap on the top bar goes back to the top.

** Notebooks
- *New notebook*: the =+= button on the home screen.
- *Long press* on a notebook: selection, to rename or delete it.
- A notebook can have an icon (an emoji) instead of its initial.
- The text before the first note (the "preface") shows at the top of the notebook; tap it to edit it.

** Notes and sub-notes
- The =+= button of a notebook adds a note at the end.
- *Swipe a note left*: 🗑 delete, then new note above, as a sub-note or below.
- *Swipe right*: to do ↔ done.
- The chevrons fold the sub-notes; *Collapse all / Expand all* at the top of the notebook. Folds stay as you leave them, also after the app is closed.
- The first time a notebook is shown, it folds as Emacs would show it: after a =#+STARTUP: overview= (or =show2levels=…) line at its top, and each note's =VISIBILITY= property (=folded=, =children=, =content=, =all=).
- A deletion can be undone with the *Undo* button of the message.

*** A sub-note
Fold and unfold "Notes and sub-notes" to see it go.

* The note screen
** Title and content
Tap the title or the content to edit it where it is shown. There is no "Save" button: the note is saved when you leave the field, close the keyboard or go back.

At the top, the path (notebook · parent notes) leads back to each of them.

** TODO State, priority, tags                                          :example:guide:
SCHEDULED: <{{date:+2}}>
Under the title, the chips add what is missing: *Tags*, *State*, *Priority*, *Scheduled*, *Deadline*, *Property*, *Clock*. Tap a value to change it.

- State: TODO, NEXT, DONE… (set in *Settings → State keywords*, or per notebook with a =#+TODO:= line, as at the top of this file).
- Priority: A, B or C, shown as =#A= before the title.
- Dates: day, time, end time, and repeater (=+1w=, =++1d=, =.+1m=).
- A repeating task marked DONE moves on to its next date.

** [#A] Important note with a deadline
DEADLINE: <{{date:+5}}>
The deadline shows in the agenda on its date, in red, then under "Overdue" once it has passed.

** Properties
:PROPERTIES:
:PLACE:    Lyon
:BUDGET:   300
:SEE:      [[*Formulas][Formulas]]
:END:
*Property* adds a name / value line. While you type, Orgwhal suggests the names already used in your notebooks, then the values already given to that name.

- A link in a value shows as a link, as =SEE= above: tap it to open it, tap beside it to edit the value.
- *Settings → Hidden properties*: the ones not to show (ID, CREATED…); they stay in the file.
- *Settings → Add an ID / a creation date to new notes*.

** The ⋮ menu of a note
- *Copy link to this note*: an =[[id:…][Title]]= link to paste elsewhere.
- *Move to…*: choose a notebook, then its end or a note to put it under.
- *Archive*: see "Archiving" below.
- *Share*: the note, its title or its content, to another app.
- The =+= button at the bottom: new note above, as a sub-note or below this one.

* Writing
** Formatting
Org is written as text and shown formatted: *bold*, /italic/, _underline_, +strike-through+, =verbatim= and ~code~.

The *toolbar* over the keyboard (while editing the content) does bold, italic, underline, strike-through, code, list, checkbox, indent / outdent, =id:= and date. Undo and redo stay on the right. While editing the title or a table's field, a shorter one leaves out lists and indents, and its date button writes an inactive date alone, as a journal's entries are titled (=*** [2026-10-13 Tue]=); *Today* writes today's. A note tagged =:template:= is a template: on a new note, the *Template* chip lists them (a search narrows them down) and starts it as a copy (title, state, tags, content), with =%u= written as today's inactive date, =%U= with the time, =%t= / =%T= active, as in Emacs' capture templates. Templates stay out of the agenda and the search (unless searched for with =t.template=); *Settings → Template notes* changes that.

Typing helps with Org's syntax: brackets close themselves; =#= at the start of a line offers the keywords (=#+transclude:=, =#+NAME:=, =#+TBLFM:=, and blocks written whole, the cursor inside); a line =:NOTES:= followed by a new line gets its =:END:=; =<<= gets its =>>= and offers the names that links wait for (written in a link, with nothing there yet), warning when the name is already used elsewhere.

** Lists and checkboxes
- [X] A checked box
- [ ] A box to check: tap it
- An item with sub-items folds with the chevron at the end of its line
  - first sub-item
  - second sub-item

While you type, a list goes on by itself on the next line; an empty item ends it.

** Progress cookies [1/3]
A =[/]= or a =[%]= in a title or a list item counts what is done. Orgwhal keeps it up to date as Emacs does, at each box checked:

- [-] Luggage [33%]
  - [X] Clothes
  - [ ] Toiletries
  - [ ] Chargers
- [X] Train tickets
- [ ] Passport

The cookie of the title counts the top-level boxes. Without boxes, it counts the sub-notes done.

** Project with steps [50%]
*** DONE Step 1
*** TODO Step 2
Swipe this step right: the cookie of the project goes to 100%.

** Dates in the text
An active timestamp in the text is an *event* and shows in the agenda: meeting <{{date:+3}} 14:00-15:00>.

An inactive timestamp just records a moment: [{{date:0}} 10:00]. The date button of the toolbar offers *Now* to insert one.

* Links
** Links to a note
Type =id:= in the content, then a few letters of a title: Orgwhal suggests the notes, and the one chosen becomes a link. A parent's title (or the notebook's) also finds the notes under it, after the titles that match. The note linked to gets an ID if it had none.

Example: [[id:{{id}}][The target note]].

At the bottom of the note linked to, *Linked from* lists the notes that point to it.

When a note is renamed, the links that still show the old title, or lead to the note by it ([[Title]], [[*Title]]), can be updated (*Settings → Links to a renamed note*). So can the links to a renamed target or =#+NAME= (=#+transclude:= lines and =remote()= formulas too).

** The target note
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       {{id}}
:END:
Open it: the note "Links to a note" shows under *Linked from*.

** Links to a notebook, a website, a phone…
- Type =file:= to link a notebook of the folder: [[file:orgwhal-guide.org][this guide]].
- Addresses open with a tap: https://orgmode.org, [[https://orgmode.org/manual/][the Org manual]].
- Also =mailto:=, =tel:= and =geo:=.

** Internal links
Without a scheme, a link leads within the notes, as in Org:

- to a target written in the text: [[meeting-point]];
- to a table or block named with =#+NAME=: [[expenses]];
- to a note by its title: [[*Tables and formulas]].

Type =[[= then a few letters: Orgwhal suggests the notes, the targets and the names, and writes the link by the title ([[Title]]). A note in another notebook is linked by its id, as Emacs looks for a title in the link's file only; so is one whose title leads elsewhere (another note has it) or has brackets.

A link to a note not there yet, such as [[*Ideas for the weekend]], offers to make it, as Emacs does, at the end of the notebook; *⋮ → Move to…* puts it elsewhere. The links to it in that notebook become =id:= links, their text kept, so that they follow the note when it is renamed.

** Content from another note
A line =#+transclude:= followed by a link shows, in its place, what the link leads to, as Emacs' org-transclusion does: a note's content, a table or block named with =#+NAME=, or the paragraph (or list item) of a target such as [[meeting-point]]. =:lines 2-5= keeps those lines only. It is read only: a tap on its title opens the note it comes from, where it is changed; the file keeps the line alone. Targets (<<name>>, shown small and grey in the text) are left out of what shows, as when exported.

#+transclude: [[expenses]]
#+transclude: [[meeting-point]]

** A target
The link "meeting-point" leads here: <<meeting-point>> in front of the station.

* Tables and formulas
** A simple table
| Fruit  | Quantity |
|--------+----------|
| Apples |        6 |
| Pears  |        4 |

Columns of numbers align right. While the note's text is edited, table lines show in a fixed-width font, so that they line up as in Emacs; *Settings → Fixed-width font* shows all notes' content in it. Long fields go on several lines so that the table fits the screen; *Settings → Wrap table fields* turns it off, the table then scrolling sideways. Every other row is tinted; in a note, the header of a long table stays at the top as you scroll down, and *Settings → Freeze the first column* keeps the first column at the left as a wide table scrolls sideways. Links can be typed in a field too: =id:= suggests notes, as in the text.

Tap a field to change it right in the table; another field, a tap elsewhere or closing the keyboard saves it, and the table realigns as Emacs writes it. *Enter* goes on to the field below, as in Emacs; on the last row it adds a new row. A long press on a field adds a row or column next to it, or deletes its own; the formulas follow. Tap elsewhere in the note to edit its text. This can be turned off in *Settings → Edit table cells on tap*.

** Formulas
Under a table, a =#+TBLFM:= line holds its formulas. The "ƒ formulas" line shows them; *Recalculate* computes them and realigns the table, as =C-c C-c= does in Emacs.

#+NAME: expenses
| Item       | Qty | Price | Total |
|------------+-----+-------+-------|
| Train      |   2 |    45 |       |
| Hotel      |   3 |    80 |       |
| Restaurant |   4 |    25 |       |
|------------+-----+-------+-------|
| Total      |     |       |       |
#+TBLFM: $4=$2*$3::@>$4=vsum(@I..@II)

Try it: tap *Recalculate*, change a quantity, then recalculate. The fields a formula computes (the totals) do not open for editing: the next recalculation would replace them.

- Column formula: =$4=$2*$3=; field formula: =@>$4=…= (last row, column 4).
- References: =$2=, =@3$2=, =@<= / =@>= (first / last row), =@-1= (row above), ranges =@2..@-1=, =@I..@II= (between two rules).
- Operations: =+ - * / ^=, parentheses, =vsum=, =vmean=, =vmin=, =vmax=, =vcount=, =abs=.
- Formats: =;%.2f= (two decimals), =;%d= (whole number).
- Lisp formulas and Calc's other functions are left as they are.

** Reading another table
=remote(name, reference)= reads a field of a named table, even in another note:

| Total budget | Per person |
|--------------+------------|
|              |            |
#+TBLFM: @2$1=remote(expenses, @>$4)::@2$2=@2$1/2;%.2f

Recalculate "Formulas", then this one.

In *Settings → Table formulas*, tables can also be recalculated as soon as a note shows them, with the tables they read: on screen only (*Save values* then writes them to the file), or saved right away. By default they are not: the file changes only when you tap *Recalculate*.

* Clocking
** Write the report
:LOGBOOK:
CLOCK: [{{date:-1}} 09:00]--[{{date:-1}} 10:30] =>  1:30
CLOCK: [{{date:-2}} 14:00]--[{{date:-2}} 14:45] =>  0:45
:END:
The *Clock* line shows the time spent (2:15 here), and beside it the last time clocked (1:30). While the clock runs, it shows the time since it started, with the total beside. Tap ▶ to start, ■ to stop; it is written to the =:LOGBOOK:= as in Emacs. In the note, tap "LOGBOOK" to see each time clocked.

- Only one clock runs at a time: starting one stops the other.
- "Clock running" shows under the note in the lists.
- Marking a note done stops its clock, as in Emacs.
- While a clock runs, a notification shows its time, with *Stop* and, for a to-do note, *Done*; it follows the file, so a clock stopped in Emacs takes it away.

* Agenda and reminders
** The agenda
Menu ☰ → *Agenda*: overdue tasks, today and the next six days. It gathers scheduled times, deadlines and the events in the text.

- Filters at the top: *All notebooks*, *All tags*.
- At the same time, notes are sorted by priority.
- Swipe right: done. Swipe left: *+1d*, *+7d* or *Choose a date*.

** TODO Water the plants
SCHEDULED: <{{date:+1}}>
A task for tomorrow, in the agenda. With a repeater such as =+3d=, it would come back every three days.

** Reminders
A notification rings at scheduled and deadline times (*Settings → Reminders*). It offers *Done*, a snooze (in 1 hour by default, set in *Settings → Snooze*) and *Clock in*, which starts the note's clock; *Settings → Third button* puts *Tomorrow* in its place. Under the title it shows the note's parent; opened, the whole path and the start of the note.

For reminders on time, see *Settings → Reliability*: notifications, exact alarms and no battery restriction; *Use alarm clock alarms* is the most reliable.

* Search
** Searching
The magnifier searches the notebook shown, or everywhere from the home screen. Type words, or Orgzly's syntax:

| Search          | Finds                                   |
|-----------------+-----------------------------------------|
| it.todo         | tasks to do                             |
| it.done         | tasks done                              |
| t.guide         | notes with the tag guide                |
| p.A             | priority A                              |
| s.today         | scheduled up to today                   |
| d.3d            | deadline within 3 days                  |
| s.none          | not scheduled                           |
| e.eq.today      | events of the day                       |
| cr.eq.today     | created today (CREATED property)        |
| b.orgwhal-guide | in this notebook (its file name)        |
| col.stock.eq.0  | a table field Stock at 0                |
| ad.7            | the results as a 7-day agenda           |
| o.p             | sort by priority (o.s, o.d, o.e, o.cr…) |

Conditions combine: =it.todo t.guide o.p=. A dot in front negates one: =.t.guide= (without the tag guide). =or= and parentheses work, and =.o.p= sorts the other way. Durations are written =3d=, =2w=, =1m=; comparisons =eq=, =lt=, =ge=…

=col.= reads tables: the field under a column so headed (in a table with a header), or else the one right of a field so named (=| Stock | 0 |=); one row matching is enough. Numbers compare as numbers (=col.pages.gt.20=), the rest as text (=col.place.eq.cellar=), the name ignoring case and accents; quotes keep spaces: =col."in stock".eq.0=.

As you type, a list proposes what comes next: the conditions from their first letter (all of them in an empty field), then your tags, notebooks, state keywords and table columns, comparisons and dates. A tap writes it in.

** Saved searches
*Save search* keeps the search in *Searches* (menu ☰), to run it again with a tap. They can be renamed, reordered and deleted.

* Multiple selection
A *long press* on a note selects it; tap others to add them.

- *Cut*, *Copy*, *Paste* (above, as a sub-note, below). A copy gets no ID, so that links to the note still lead to it; cut notes keep theirs.
- *Move*: up, down, one level up or down, *Move to…*
- Set the state, scheduled time or deadline of all of them at once.
- *Select all*, *Archive*, *Delete* (with undo).

* Tree view
In a notebook, *⋮ → Tree view* draws its notes as boxes from left to right.

- Move it with a finger, zoom with two; a *double tap* between the boxes zooms in, then back to the whole tree.
- A tap on a box opens the note; the round button at its side folds its sub-notes and shows how many are hidden.
- *Collapse all / Expand all* and *Show the whole tree* at the top.
- A *long press* selects notes, with the same bars as in the notebook: move them up, down, a level up or down, and they slide to their new places.
- The tree comes back as you left it: zoom, place and folds.

* Archiving
*⋮ → Archive* on a note (or on a selection) moves it with its sub-notes to the end of the notebook's archive file, next to it: =orgwhal-guide.org_archive= for this one. As in Emacs (=C-c C-x C-s=), it gets properties saying when and where from (=ARCHIVE_TIME=, =ARCHIVE_FILE=, =ARCHIVE_OLPATH=…).

The archive file is not a notebook: archived notes leave the lists, the agenda and the search, and the notebook stays light. Open the file in Emacs to read them again. The message offers to undo it.

* Sharing to Orgwhal
From another app (browser, notes…), *Share → Orgwhal* makes a note in the *Inbox* notebook (made when needed): the page title as title, the link as content.

On that new note, tap *Inbox ▾* at the top to put it in another notebook, under the note of your choice. An Inbox left empty goes away.

* Useful settings
- *Appearance*: light, dark or system theme; wallpaper colors; *Use English language*.
- *Notes*: show note content in notebooks, strike through done notes, space between notes, sub-note indentation (wide, tapering or compact), state keywords, hidden properties.
- *About*: version, source code, license, and *Add the guide notebook* to get this notebook back, or to replace it with a newer version's after an update.
